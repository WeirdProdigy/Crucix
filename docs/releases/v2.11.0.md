### Tíz új élő forrás

Mindegyikhez nem kell kulcs vagy fiók. A kérések korlátosak (10 s időkorlát, nincs újrapróbálás, legfeljebb 2 MiB törzs; az OpenSanctions-indexnél 3 MiB), a lassan változó forrásoknál korlátos, memóriabeli gyorsítótár van, és minden sor a szolgáltató idejét mutatja, soha nem a gyűjtését. A „frissesség” két érték: a **feed** a szolgáltatói adatfolyam megengedett kora, a **rekord** egy sor megengedett kora. Amelyik forrás hibázik, az csak a saját kártyáján lesz `error`; a sweepet nem állítja meg. A sorok súlyossága a szokásos szavakkal adott (`critical`, `high`, `moderate`, `low`, `monitor`, `info`); a Rekordvizsgálóban és a riasztásokban ◆ KRITIKUS, ▲ MAGAS, ● FIGYELENDŐ, ○ TÁJÉKOZTATÓ.

- **IMF PortWatch** (`IMF-PortWatch`, típus `maritime`). **Mit mér:** becsült napi hajóáthaladás műholdas AIS-jelek alapján nyolc tengeri szorosnál: Hormuzi-szoros, Báb-el-Mandeb, Szuezi-csatorna, Malaccai-szoros, Boszporusz, Panama-csatorna, Gibraltári-szoros és Dover-szoros (`publicSources.portwatchChokepoints`, legfeljebb 12 név, pontosan úgy írva, ahogy az IMF réteg írja). Szorosonként egy sor, a legutóbb közölt napról: a réteg hetente frissül és 2–9 nappal késik, ezért a sor sosem a mai forgalom. **Súlyosság:** a 7 napos átlag (a 7 napból legalább 5 kell) a megelőző 28 nap mediánjához mérve (legalább 14 nap kell): −50% vagy rosszabb `high`, −25% vagy rosszabb `moderate`, különben `info`; a szorost csak akkor minősíti, ha ez az alap legalább 10 áthaladás/nap, alatta a sor „nem minősített”. (Az első, egynapos változat a Hormuzi-szorost napi 5 áthaladásos mediánnál 105 napból 32-n `high` szintre tette, ezért vetettem el.) **Tények és mutatók:** a nap darabszáma (`transitCalls`), `mean7d`, `baseline28d`, `changePct`; a `<szoros>_transits` mutató a 7 napos átlag (nincs, ha 5 napnál kevesebb áll rendelkezésre). **Hely és előzmény:** a sor a szoros helyén jelenik meg; a link szorosonként és naponként egyedi (`…/pages/<id>?date=<nap>`), így az előzmény szorosonként és naponként egy rekordot tart. **Kérések:** sweepenként kettő (napi réteg; az IMF szorosrétege a koordinátákért), a válasz 6 óráig gyorsítótárazott. **Frissesség:** feed 10 nap, rekord 10 nap. **Licenc:** „IMF terms of use: personal, noncommercial usage only, without any right to resell or redistribute”; forrásmegjelölés: „Sources: UN Global Platform; IMF PortWatch (portwatch.imf.org)”. Az IMF feltételoldala nem böngésző kliensnek 403-at ad, ezért a Crucix a szöveget nem tudta automatikusan ellenőrizni; óvatosan, nem kereskedelmi szövegként idézi. **Mi NEM ez:** csak azokat a hajókat számolja, amelyek AIS-jelét veszik; a sötét vagy AIS-t kikapcsoló hajók nem szerepelnek, és ez becslés, nem élő pozíció.

(48,7 napnyi valós eseményben, napi kb. 20-szal, a leghosszabb szünet 9,2 óra volt; a 6 órás korlát az adatfolyamot az idő kb. 0,8%-ában lejártnak mutatta volna.) A paraméterek előzetesek, módosulhatnak.

### Keretrendszer

- **A kilencforrásos korlát megszűnt.** A szerveroldali regiszter (`normalizeLiveSources`), a frissességi szabályok böngészői másolata (`live-sources.js`, kétszer) és a dashboard pillanatkép-normalizálója eddig kilenc elemnél vágta a listát; most a regisztrált források számát használják, ami 19. A szabálytábla, a kezdőlapok, a tényekhez tartozó mezők és a nyelvi tény-kulcsok egyezését mind a 19 forrásra teszt őrzi, és a böngészői szabálymásolatnak azonosnak kell lennie a szerveréivel.
- **Hat új élő típus:** `earthquake`, `maritime`, `aviation`, `sanctions`, `market`, `energy`. Az előzménynapló típuslistája ugyanebből a listából származik; a típusok HU/EN/FR címkét kaptak, és az Előzmények típusszűrője kínálja, a Rekordvizsgáló pedig címkézi őket.

> **Before you upgrade: three things worth knowing**
>
> - **Events of the new sources can open alerts too.** The new rows join the existing events in the alert engine, so the built-in `events-critical` and `events-high` rules (both notify) also open alerts for HIGH or CRITICAL rows of the new sources: for example an M6+ earthquake (EMSC), a volcanic-ash SIGMET in force, an aircraft squawking 7700 or 7500, a power price of 400 EUR/MWh or more. The silent baseline exists only at the very first start of the alert engine, so the first sweep after the upgrade can send notifications about whatever is in force right then. The number of notifications is limited by `ALERT_MAX_NOTIFICATIONS_PER_SWEEP` (default 5, the rest in a single summary). The built-in rules can be overridden in the **Rules** tab.
> - **Three sources are, by their terms, for non-commercial use.** IMF PortWatch (personal, non-commercial use), OpenSanctions (CC BY-NC 4.0) and Manifold (personal and non-commercial use). If you use Crucix commercially, read their terms; the licences of the others are more open (see the per-source list).
> - **The "Current public data" panel is now 19 cards and therefore tall,** and the PWA offline snapshot can in an extreme case exceed a 5 MiB limit (see the known limits).

### Ten new live sources

None needs a key or an account. Requests are bounded (10 s timeout, no retries, at most 2 MiB body; 3 MiB for the OpenSanctions index), the slowly changing sources keep a bounded in-memory cache, and every row shows the provider's time, never the collection time. "Freshness" has two values: the **feed** is the permitted age of the provider's feed, the **record** is the permitted age of one row. A source that fails turns into an `error` on its own card only; it never stops the sweep. Row severity uses the usual words (`critical`, `high`, `moderate`, `low`, `monitor`, `info`); in the Record Inspector and in alerts they read ◆ CRITICAL, ▲ HIGH, ● WATCH, ○ INFO.

- **IMF PortWatch** (`IMF-PortWatch`, kind `maritime`). **What it measures:** estimated daily ship transits, from satellite AIS signals, at eight chokepoints: Strait of Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, Suez Canal, Malacca Strait, Bosporus, Panama Canal, Gibraltar Strait and Dover Strait (`publicSources.portwatchChokepoints`, at most 12 names, spelled exactly as the IMF layer spells them). One row per chokepoint for the latest day the IMF has published: the layer is refreshed weekly and runs 2–9 days behind, so a row is never today's traffic. **Severity:** the 7-day mean (at least 5 of the 7 days are needed) against the median of the previous 28 days (at least 14 days are needed): −50% or worse is `high`, −25% or worse is `moderate`, otherwise `info`; a strait is rated only when that baseline is at least 10 transits a day, below it the row says "not rated". (The first, single-day version rated Hormuz `high` on 32 of 105 days at a median of 5 transits a day, so it was dropped.) **Facts and metrics:** the day's count (`transitCalls`), `mean7d`, `baseline28d`, `changePct`; the `<slug>_transits` metric is the 7-day mean (absent when fewer than 5 days are available). **Location and history:** the row sits at the chokepoint; the link is unique per chokepoint and day (`…/pages/<id>?date=<day>`), so history keeps one record per chokepoint and day. **Requests:** two per sweep (the daily layer; the IMF's chokepoint layer for the coordinates), answers cached 6 h. **Freshness:** feed 10 days, record 10 days. **Licence:** "IMF terms of use: personal, noncommercial usage only, without any right to resell or redistribute"; attribution "Sources: UN Global Platform; IMF PortWatch (portwatch.imf.org)". The IMF terms page answers 403 to non-browser clients, so Crucix could not check the wording automatically; it is quoted conservatively, as non-commercial. **What it is NOT:** it counts only ships whose AIS signal is received; dark or AIS-off ships are not counted, and it is an estimate, not a live position.
- **EMSC** (`EMSC`, kind `earthquake`). **What it measures:** earthquakes of magnitude 4.5 or larger reported in the last 24 hours by the EMSC-CSEM SeismicPortal FDSN service; a single request (`limit=101`, ordered by magnitude, so a very busy day can cut only the smallest), at most 100 rows, ranked by magnitude. Only known or suspected earthquakes count (explosions and other types do not). **Severity:** M7 or more `critical`, M6 or more `high`, M5 or more `moderate`, otherwise `low`. **Facts:** magnitude, depth. **Freshness:** the service has no feed time, so the newest event time stands in: feed 12 hours, event 26 hours. (In 48.7 days of real events, about 20 a day, the longest gap was 9.2 hours; a 6-hour limit would have read the feed as expired about 0.8% of the time.) An empty answer (HTTP 204) carries no provider time and reads stale, never "ok, zero events". **Licence:** datasets of the SeismicPortal FDSN service are CC BY 4.0, credit EMSC-CSEM; the service and databases themselves are not covered, and commercial reproduction needs EMSC's written permission. Parameters are preliminary and can be revised.
- **Copernicus EMS** (`Copernicus-EMS`, kind `disaster`). **What it measures:** rapid-mapping activations from the public list of the service's dashboard (`mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu`; the endpoint is the one the site itself uses and it is not documented). Up to 20 activations of the last 30 days, newest first (the request is `limit=30`), at the centroid the list gives (an approximate location). The service publishes no severity, so every row is `moderate`. The observation is the activation time (UTC); when checked on 2026-10-02 (265 activations) every listed activation was closed, so rows lag the disaster by days. **Freshness:** record 30 days, feed 45 days (the gap between two activations reached 37.8 days in 3.5 years, so a 30-day feed limit read stale). The answer is cached 1 h. **Licence:** free, full and open access under Regulation (EU) 2021/696, naming the source (Copernicus EMS terms, 07/06/2023); only public activation details are used, not the mapping products.
- **Aviation SIGMET** (`Aviation-SIGMET`, kind `weather`). **What it measures:** international SIGMETs in force (volcanic ash, severe turbulence and icing, tropical cyclones, thunderstorms, mountain wave, dust and sandstorms) from the NOAA/NWS Aviation Weather Center. Up to 100 rows, ranked by hazard (volcanic ash `high`; severe turbulence, severe icing and tropical cyclone `moderate`; the rest `low`), then newest first. Only SIGMETs that have started and not yet ended appear (the end is the row's `validUntil`). One row per hazard area; the "…F" forecast twin of a message is dropped. The position is the centre of the area (approximate; for a degenerate or self-crossing area the mean of its points, labelled accordingly); the bulletin text is the authority. SIGMETs that the United States issues in its domestic format are not in this service. **Freshness:** feed 3 hours (the newest receipt time), record 24 hours. **Link:** the AWC map page with a unique `sigmet=<id>` parameter, because AWC has no page per SIGMET and history merges rows that share a link. **Licence:** public domain (NWS disclaimer); AWC asks for a custom User-Agent and at most 100 requests a minute. **Limit:** areas across the antimeridian carry longitudes beyond ±180; those points are dropped, so the position of such an area can be off (2 of 150 live rows when checked).
- **ADSB-Military** (`ADSB-Military`, kind `aviation`). **What it measures:** military air activity from adsb.lol, a community network of ADS-B receivers. **Aggregates, not aircraft:** per watched theater (`publicSources.adsbTheaters`; by default Black Sea and Ukraine, Eastern Mediterranean, Middle East and Gulf, Baltic Sea, South China Sea and Taiwan, Korean Peninsula, Central Europe; at most 12 boxes; an aircraft counts in the first box that contains it) one row with the number of military aircraft that are airborne and reported a position in the last 2 minutes, shown at the box centre. In addition, every aircraft anywhere that transmits an emergency squawk (7700 and 7500 `high`, 7600 `moderate`; up to 20, at the real position). A theater row is `monitor` ("surge") when it holds at least 10 aircraft and at least three times the count of this process's previous sweep (the previous sweep may be at most 6 hours old and is lost on a restart). The `mil_aircraft_total` metric is all airborne military aircraft with a current position, worldwide. **Not a full picture:** ADS-B-visible aircraft only; military aircraft with transponders off and areas without receivers do not appear, so a low count is no proof of low activity; a squawk code is set by the crew and is not a confirmed emergency. **Stall guard:** an empty list, or one without any airborne aircraft with a current position, is an error (no metric of 0). **Requests and limits:** adsb.lol rate-limits hard (a fourth request within about 3 seconds of the previous one gets HTTP 429), so a sweep asks the military list first, then 7700 and 7500 together and 7600 after a 10 s pause (if it fits the 26 s budget): at most 4 requests. A manual `/sweep` within a minute of a scheduled one can get a 429; the card then shows an error until the next sweep. **Freshness:** 25 minutes. **Link:** the adsb.lol map with `&theater=<id>` (unique per row, the map ignores it) or `?icao=<hex>`. **Licence:** ODbL 1.0, credit adsb.lol, share derived databases alike; the API is free today, its terms say a key may be required in the future and ask production users to get in touch. The surge rule rarely fires: when first checked the list held 239 aircraft worldwide (about three quarters over the United States) and 5 inside the default theaters.
- **OpenSanctions index** (`OpenSanctions-Index`, kind `sanctions`). **What it measures:** when six sanctions lists last changed and how many entries they hold: OFAC SDN, EU Financial Sanctions Files, UN Security Council, UK FCDO, US BIS Denied Persons and the US Trade Consolidated Screening List (`us_ofac_sdn`, `eu_fsf`, `un_sc_sanctions`, `gb_fcdo_sanctions`, `us_bis_denied`, `us_trade_csl`), from the OpenSanctions dataset index in one request (the index is about 2 MiB, a few hundred bytes over the 2 MiB request limit, so this source allows 3 MiB; the answer is cached 1 h). One row per list that changed in the last 14 days, newest first; each change is its own record (the id and the `?change=<stamp>` link carry the change time; the site ignores the parameter). `moderate` when the list grew by at least 10 entries since this server's previous sweep; otherwise `info`, so large delistings are not rated; there is no change value after a restart. **Facts:** entry count and change. **Freshness:** feed 48 hours (the index's own run time), record 14 days. **What it is NOT:** the change time is OpenSanctions' view of the list, not the issuer's publication time; an index that arrives with renamed fields reads as an error, not as a quiet feed. **Licence:** CC BY-NC 4.0; OpenSanctions says "OpenSanctions is free for non-commercial users. Businesses must acquire a data license".
- **Federal Register** (`Federal-Register`, kind `sanctions`). **What it measures:** the newest rules and notices of the Treasury's OFAC and the Commerce Department's BIS, from the public documents API: two requests (the 15 newest per agency, cached 1 h), up to 30 rows, newest first. Rules (`Rule`) whose title mentions the Entity List or designations are `moderate`, everything else `info`. A publication day counts as 00:00 UTC. **Freshness:** feed and record both 14 days (a 96-hour feed limit would have read expired 21–26% of the time over the last 12 months, a 336-hour one 2.2%; the longest gap was 22 days, during the October 2025 shutdown). Each document links to its own federalregister.gov page. If one agency's request fails, the other's documents stay and the summary says so. **Licence:** public domain (1 CFR 2.6); the federalregister.gov rendition is unofficial, the official PDF is on govinfo.gov.
- **Energy-Charts HU** (`Energy-Charts-HU`, kind `energy`). **What it measures:** three rows from the Fraunhofer ISE Energy-Charts API: the Hungarian day-ahead power price, the grid frequency of Continental Europe and the renewable share of Hungarian generation; three requests per sweep, each answer cached 2 minutes (the provider allows 2 requests a minute per endpoint). **Price:** the newest 15-minute day-ahead slot that has started, EUR/MWh; the day-ahead auction result, fixed a day ahead, not a real-time price. **Severity:** price `high` from 400 EUR/MWh, `moderate` from 300, otherwise `info`; frequency by its deviation from 50 Hz: `high` from 0.2 Hz, `moderate` from 0.1 Hz; the share is `info`. The price thresholds follow a measurement: over the 14 days 2026-09-19 to 2026-10-02 (1,344 15-minute slots) the Hungarian median was 193.88 EUR/MWh, 72.4% of the slots were at or above 150, only 3.9% at or above 300 and 0.6% at or above 400; the 300/150 thresholds of the first design would have rated most of the day. The frequency is measured in Freiburg (Germany) for the synchronous area Continental Europe, which Hungary belongs to, about 90 seconds behind; the generation data lag about an hour and a half. **Metrics:** `hu_power_price`, `grid_frequency_hz`. The price answer carries its own licence field and is published only if it says CC BY 4.0. **Freshness:** 6 hours. **Licence:** CC BY 4.0, credit energy-charts.info; prices: Bundesnetzagentur | SMARD.de; commercial access needs a contact with Fraunhofer ISE.
- **ENTSOG HU** (`ENTSOG-HU`, kind `energy`). **What it measures:** physical gas flow (ENTSOG Transparency Platform, "Physical Flow") at the seven Hungarian cross-border points: Dravaszerdahely (HU/HR), Balassagyarmat (HU/SK), Csanadpalota (HU/RO), Mosonmagyarovar (HU/AT), Kiskundorozsma (HU/RS, a one-way exit), Kiskundorozsma-2 / Horgos (the TurkStream entry) and VIP Bereg (HU/UA). One row per point for the latest completed gas day (06:00 to 06:00 CET/CEST), as reported by the Hungarian transmission operator FGSZ, so entry is gas arriving in Hungary and exit is gas leaving it. The gas day in progress, and a day that was last updated before it ended, are not used. Values are provisional (`Provisional` on every record seen) and can be revised. Severity is always `info`, there is no metric. **Requests:** one an hour (a single request for all points, cached 1 h), because the terms forbid usage that reduces the platform's performance. **Freshness:** 72 hours. **Terms and a proviso:** the ENTSOG Transparency Platform terms (art. 5) allow download and use in good faith with the source and the download date cited (the card carries "ENTSOG TP DD-MM-YYYY https://transparency.entsog.eu/"); "ENTSOG does not permit automatic extraction of data or other usage that reduces the performance of the ENTSOG TP", hence the hourly limit; ENTSOG accepts no liability for accuracy or timeliness. **Link:** the API answer (JSON) for that point and gas day, the only verifiable deep link, not a page meant for people.
- **Prediction markets** (`Prediction-Markets`, kind `market`, Manifold only). **What it measures:** the probability that traders on Manifold, a play-money prediction market, give to questions that match the watched words (`publicSources.marketQueries`; by default Hormuz, Ukraine, Iran, Taiwan, recession; every word has to start a word of the question, ignoring letter case and accents; at most 6 entries, one request each: 5 by default, answers cached 30 minutes). Up to 20 rows, most traded first. Only a market that is not closed, binary and play money, with a bet in the last 12 hours, is listed; the observation is the time of the last bet; a market nobody has bet on is left out (its probability is the creator's starting value); a row expires at the market's close (`validUntil`; a market that closes in 2100 or later shows no close date). Always `info`. **Facts:** probability, volume (Mana), close, platform. **What it is NOT:** a market probability is the opinion of traders, not an event; the volume is play money (Mana has no cash value). **Licence:** Manifold Terms of Service: personal and non-commercial use, API automation allowed, commercial use needs a data licence. Kalshi was skipped (see below).

### Framework changes

- **The nine-source limit is gone.** The server-side registry (`normalizeLiveSources`), the browser copy of the freshness policies (`live-sources.js`, twice) and the dashboard's snapshot normaliser used to cut the list at nine; they now use the number of registered sources, 19. A test keeps the policy table, the home pages, the fact fields and the locale fact keys in agreement for all 19, and the browser copy of the policies must equal the server's.
- **Six new live kinds:** `earthquake`, `maritime`, `aviation`, `sanctions`, `market`, `energy`. The history's kind list is derived from the same list; the kinds have HU/EN/FR labels, and the History kind filter offers them and the Record Inspector labels them.
- **Links and history:** history merges records of the same kind and the same link, so every new row has a link of its own. Where the provider has one page for several rows (the AWC map, PortWatch, the adsb.lol map, the OpenSanctions dataset page) the link carries a unique query parameter (`sigmet=`, `date=`, `theater=`, `change=`); the AWC, adsb.lol and OpenSanctions pages ignored it when checked.
- **Location-method labels:** the Record Inspector now says the new methods in plain language: `theater-centre` (the centre of the watched area, an aggregate, not a position), `polygon-centroid`, `polygon-vertex-mean` and `global`. An older gap is fixed too: MET Norway rows showed the raw `configured-point` and `forecast-grid` tokens although the locale keys existed.
- **Configuration:** `publicSources` in `crucix.config.mjs` gets three new optional lists (`portwatchChokepoints`, `adsbTheaters`, `marketQueries`) with sensible defaults; when one is missing the default applies. There is no new mandatory environment variable. `apis/briefing.mjs` runs 50 sources (it was 40), and the `package.json` description and the README counts follow.
- **QA and fixture:** the browser QA has a new `live` phase (19 cards on desktop and mobile, the history filter for the new kinds, map markers), and the fixture provides a sample for every source, driven by `POLICIES`.

### Map

- **A layer by kind:** located live rows appear in the layer of their kind: `earthquake` in the earthquake layer, `maritime` in the maritime chokepoints layer, `aviation` in the air layer, `disaster` in current natural events, `weather` and `forecast` in the weather layer. Kinds without a location (`sanctions`, `market`, `energy`) draw no marker. On the globe and on the flat map.
- **Colours:** a located live row takes the colour of its layer: earthquake and natural event orange, maritime purple, air teal, the rest the usual blue. (Until now the flat map drew every located live row blue.)
- **USGS/EMSC: drawn once.** An EMSC earthquake within 60 seconds and 100 km of a USGS earthquake is not drawn separately; the USGS marker stays. Both events remain in the lists, in history and in alerts; the de-duplication happens only when drawing. In the real sweep the same M4.7 near Korumburra, Australia appeared in both catalogues: USGS gave 16:01:39.581 UTC at −38.3802, 145.8401 and EMSC gave 16:01:39.630 UTC at −38.3803, 145.8704, that is 0.05 s and 2.6 km apart.
- **Label:** the earthquake layer toggle reads "Earthquakes · USGS significant + EMSC M4.5+" (HU/EN/FR), because it now draws both catalogues. The Sensor Grid "Earthquakes" tile still counts USGS only (sub-label "USGS significant · last 24h").

### Alerts

- **Eleven new metrics** (the alert engine now has 34, up from 23 in 2.10), offered by the rules editor with HU/EN/FR names: `hormuz_transits`, `bab_el_mandeb_transits`, `suez_transits`, `malacca_transits`, `bosporus_transits`, `panama_transits`, `gibraltar_transits`, `dover_transits` (PortWatch 7-day mean, transits a day), `hu_power_price` (EUR/MWh), `grid_frequency_hz` (Hz) and `mil_aircraft_total` (military aircraft worldwide, airborne, position within 2 minutes). A metric has a value only while its source is current; for a stale, failing or missing source it is empty and a threshold rule does not fire on it.
- **Event kinds in the rules editor:** `maritime`, `aviation`, `sanctions`, `market` and `energy` can be ticked too.
- **The new rows in event rules:** events of the new sources fall under the event rules like all others (except kind `signal`). The kind list of the default convergence rule (`conflict`, `earthquake`, `weather`, `disaster`, `outage`, `health`) is unchanged, but EMSC earthquakes (`earthquake`), SIGMETs (`weather`) and Copernicus activations (`disaster`) now count from WATCH up; `maritime`, `aviation`, `sanctions`, `market` and `energy` can be named in the rule's `kinds` parameter.
- **Watching for a Hormuz collapse:** PortWatch does not rate straits below 10 transits a day. In the release sweep the 7-day mean of the Strait of Hormuz was 3.1 transits a day, so it never gets a percentage rating; a threshold rule on the `hormuz_transits` metric signals a collapse (you choose the level).
- **A threshold rule for the power price:** the Hungarian day-ahead price runs high (see Energy-Charts HU), so give a `hu_power_price` threshold rule its own level; the row's own severity signals only from 300 (`moderate`) and 400 (`high`).
- **The surge rule rarely fires** (see ADSB-Military); among `aviation` rows the emergency squawks and the aggregates are what matter in practice.

### Skipped sources

- **GPSJam:** the H3 cell to coordinate conversion does not fit without a new dependency.
- **Danube water level:** a fragile HTML table.
- **Digitraffic and AISStream:** need a persistent connection or a separate collector process.
- **Cloudflare BGP:** needs a key.
- **Polymarket:** not reachable from Hungary.
- **UCDP and ACLED:** need a key.
- **Kalshi (skipped during implementation, with evidence):** the public API has no keyword or topic filter; more than 60,000 open markets were read, 51% of the 14,144 open events are sports, the first "Hormuz" match sat at position 3,924; a page of 1,000 rows is 2.2 MB, over the 2 MiB limit; and by its documentation `updated_time` tracks non-trading changes only, so it is no time of a price. Showing random markets would mislead, so the prediction-market source is Manifold only.

### Unchanged and known limits

- **Unchanged:** the nine existing live sources, the "current data only" rule (an expired or undated record is not shown), the cards, the Record Inspector and event handling, the markings of releases A and B. The server starts without a network. There is no new runtime dependency, key or account.
- **The PWA offline snapshot:** the PWA does not save a snapshot above 5 MiB; its existing "Local snapshot storage unavailable" message appears, the previously saved snapshot stays and the online dashboard is unaffected. With real data this is not expected: about 1.8 MiB with every source's longest real row copied up to its row cap, and the `/api/data` snapshot of a real sweep was 818 KB. In a synthetic worst case (every source at its row cap, texts at realistic maximum lengths) the live part is about 4.78 MiB and, with the rest of the snapshot, about 5.3 MiB, which the save would refuse. The chain test bounds the live part under 5 MiB, not the whole snapshot. This is a known limit; degrading the offline snapshot gracefully belongs to the planned dashboard-structure sub-project.
- **The panel is long:** with 19 cards the live panel is about 3900 px tall at desktop width and the right column runs far below the others. Compacting the cards belongs to the planned dashboard-structure sub-project.
- **Only AIS- and ADS-B-visible:** PortWatch does not see dark or AIS-off ships, ADSB-Military does not see aircraft flying with transponders off; neither is a live position (PortWatch lags, ADSB-Military aggregates).
- **adsb.lol can answer 429** to a manual sweep started within a minute of a scheduled one.
- **The IMF PortWatch licence wording cannot be checked automatically** (the IMF terms page answers 403 to non-browser clients); it is quoted conservatively, as non-commercial.
- **The link of ENTSOG rows** is the API's JSON answer, not a page meant for people.
- **The position of SIGMET areas across the antimeridian can be off** (see above).
- **The frequency is measured in Freiburg** for the synchronous area, not in Hungary; the Hungarian generation data lag about an hour and a half.
